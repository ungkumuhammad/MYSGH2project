# PAFC and MCFC OEMs for Stationary/Utility-Scale Power Generation

## What product lines exist for each OEM (model names, power range, stack/module configuration, operating temperature, fuel flexibility, hydrogen capability)?

### Takeaway
FuelCell Energy (US) is the dominant active MCFC OEM with the SureSource platform (300 kW–3.7 MW per unit, arrayed in multi-unit "parks" up to 58.8 MW); Doosan Fuel Cell / HyAxiom (South Korea, formerly ClearEdge Energy / UTC Power) is the dominant active PAFC OEM with the PureCell Model 400 (440–460 kW per module); Fuji Electric (Japan) is a smaller, longstanding PAFC player with 100 kW-class units. MCFC operates at ~550–650°C (no precious-metal catalyst needed, internally reforms natural gas); PAFC operates at ~150–220°C (needs Pt catalyst, tolerant of CO from reforming). Both can accept hydrogen-containing fuel streams; direct/near-pure hydrogen operation is offered by HyAxiom's PureCell M400 Hydrogen model, while FuelCell Energy's carbonate platform today runs on NG/biogas/NG-H2 blends up to 40–50% H2, with 100% pure-H2 operation reserved for its in-development solid oxide (SOFC) line, not the current SureSource carbonate product.

### Inferences
- The two OEMs occupy different technology niches: FuelCell Energy's MCFC scales via multi-module "parks" (kW-scale modules aggregated to tens of MW, as at GGE's 58.8 MW site), whereas Doosan/HyAxiom's PAFC is deployed as discrete ~440–460 kW building-block units for distributed/behind-the-meter CHP rather than utility-scale single sites.
- Direct pure-hydrogen feed is commercially offered today by HyAxiom (PAFC, M400 Hydrogen model) but not yet by FuelCell Energy's currently-shipping MCFC product; FuelCell Energy's path to pure H2 is via a separate, still-developing SOFC line, which is directly relevant to this project's need for a fuel cell that can consume the cracked, high-purity H2 output at MMHE/Singapore rather than reformed natural gas.
- Fuji Electric appears to remain a smaller-scale, Japan-centric PAFC player (100 kW class, CHP/hospital/hotel applications) rather than a utility-scale competitor to FuelCell Energy or HyAxiom; no evidence found of MW-class Fuji Electric PAFC parks.

## What is the electrical efficiency (LHV/HHV stated) and CHP/total efficiency for each product?

### Takeaway
HyAxiom's PureCell M400 discloses the clearest efficiency breakdown by fuel: ~43% electrical / ~90% total (CHP) on natural gas, ~41% electrical / ~90% total on LPG/NG dual fuel, and ~50% electrical / ~85% total on hydrogen. FuelCell Energy's SureSource 4000 MCFC is cited at ~60% electrical efficiency (basis — LHV/HHV — not stated in the source found). Fuji Electric's FP-100E is cited at 40% efficiency on an explicitly stated LHV basis. None of the sources found specify HHV-basis efficiency for any of these products; where a basis is not stated by the OEM, that is flagged.

### Cited Findings
- HyAxiom PureCell M400 (natural gas model): total efficiency 90%, electrical efficiency 43%, heat efficiency 47% — [HyAxiom/Doosan M400 datasheets, aggregated via search](https://www.doosan.com/images/media-center/ces2024/(Data%20Sheet)%20HyAxiom%20PureCell%20Model%20%20LPG%20NG.pdf) — basis (LHV/HHV) not stated in source found; flag as gap.
- HyAxiom PureCell M400 (hydrogen model): total efficiency 85%, electrical efficiency 50%, heat efficiency 35% — [HyAxiom PureCell M400 Hydrogen datasheet](https://www.doosan.com/images/media-center/ces2024/(Data%20Sheet)%20HyAxiom%20PureCell%20Model%20400%20Hydrogen.pdf) — basis not stated in source found.
- HyAxiom PureCell M400 (LPG/NG dual model): total efficiency 90%, electrical efficiency 41%, heat efficiency 49% — [HyAxiom PureCell LPG/NG datasheet](https://www.doosan.com/images/media-center/ces2024/(Data%20Sheet)%20HyAxiom%20PureCell%20Model%20%20LPG%20NG.pdf) — basis not stated in source found.
- HyAxiom/Doosan general marketing claim: PAFC technology achieves electrical efficiencies "up to 50%" in hydrogen mode and total CHP efficiencies "up to 90%" — [FCHEA, "HyAxiom - Doosan Fuel Cell America"](https://fchea.org/fchea_members/hyaxiom-doosan-fuel-cell-america/) — corroborates the M400 Hydrogen datasheet figures above; basis not stated.
- PureCell system credited with "industry-leading 10-year cell stack life" — [FCHEA, "HyAxiom - Doosan Fuel Cell America"](https://fchea.org/fchea_members/hyaxiom-doosan-fuel-cell-america/)
- FuelCell Energy SureSource 4000: electrical efficiency of approximately 60% — [FuelCell Energy — Carbonate Fuel Cell Power Plants](https://www.fuelcellenergy.com/platform/fuel-cell-power-plants) (via search aggregation) — basis (LHV/HHV) not stated in source found; flag as gap. This figure is notably higher than the ~43–50% seen for PAFC, consistent with MCFC's higher operating temperature enabling better thermodynamic efficiency, but the exact basis should be verified before use in any techno-economic comparison.
- Fuji Electric FP-100E: efficiency of 40%, explicitly stated as LHV (low heat value) basis — [OSTI/ETDEWEB record, "100-kW fuel cell power generation equipment (FP-100E)"](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20085065)

### Inferences
- The PAFC total-efficiency figures (85–90%) are CHP (combined heat+power) numbers, not electrical-only; electrical-only PAFC efficiency in the sources found ranges ~41–50% depending on fuel, with hydrogen fuel giving the highest electrical efficiency (50%) but lowest heat recovery (35%) — makes intuitive sense since less reforming-related heat loss occurs on pure H2.
- FuelCell Energy's MCFC electrical efficiency (~60%) exceeds HyAxiom's PAFC electrical efficiency (41–50%) by a wide margin, consistent with MCFC's higher operating temperature and different thermodynamic cycle; this is a first-order signal that MCFC may be the more attractive pure power-generation technology where heat recovery is not needed (e.g., grid power/data centers), while PAFC's edge is CHP total efficiency and direct hydrogen capability in a smaller unit size.

This should be requested directly from FuelCell Energy/HyAxiom spec sheets if firm numbers are needed for downstream LCOH modeling.
