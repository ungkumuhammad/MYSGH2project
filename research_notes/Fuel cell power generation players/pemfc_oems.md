# PEMFC OEMs — Stationary/Large-Scale Power Generation

## Which OEMs are commercially active in STATIONARY PEMFC power generation, and what are their product lines / model names?

### Takeaway
Plug Power (GenSure/GenSure HP), Ballard Power Systems (FCgen/FCwave-derived stationary modules), Cummins/Accelera (ex-Hydrogenics PEM stationary systems), Hyundai/HTWO (100 kW grid-tied PEMFC generator), Panasonic (ENE-FARM residential PEFC-CHP), and Toshiba Energy Systems (H2Rex) are all commercially active, with real (non-hypothetical) product lines distinct from their automotive/mobility stacks. Siemens Energy references PEMFC stationary power capability but public product-level detail (model name, spec sheet) could not be found. Singapore-headquartered Horizon Fuel Cell Technologies also sells small/mid-size stationary PEM systems (HFC-13/-40/-65, T-1000).

### Cited Findings
- **Plug Power — GenSure / GenSure HP**: GenSure HP is built from Plug Power's 125 kW ProGen fuel cell engines, arrayed in parallel; initial GenSure HP configurations are 500 kW, 1 MW and 1.5 MW, housed in standard ISO containers, and the platform is described as modular/scalable from ~100 kW to multiple MW — [Plug Power GenSure resource](https://resources.plugpower.com/gensure-stationary-power-fuel-cell); confirmed via [H2 View — Plug Power launches GenSure HP fuel cell platform](https://www.h2-view.com/story/plug-power-launches-gensure-hp-fuel-cell-platform/).

### Inferences
- The PEMFC-for-stationary-power landscape splits into three tiers: (1) MW-scale modular gensets built from stacked ~100–140 kW modules (Plug Power GenSure HP, Ballard FCgen-XD/FCwave-derived, Hydrogenics/Accelera 1 MW Korea unit) aimed at data centers/grid support; (2) sub-100 kW distributed/backup units (Hyundai HTWO 100 kW, Toshiba H2Rex 3.5–100 kW building blocks, Horizon HFC-13/40/65); (3) sub-1 kW residential micro-CHP (Panasonic ENE-FARM), a fundamentally different, much lower power-density, natural-gas/LPG-fed product category.
- Toshiba's approach of "stacking" identical 100 kW H2Rex modules (5× to reach 500 kW) mirrors Plug Power's approach of stacking 125 kW ProGen engines to reach 500 kW–1.5 MW — this modular-block architecture appears to be the industry-standard route to MW-scale rather than building large monolithic stacks.
- Given data center-scale demand (multi-MW, continuous or near-continuous duty), OEMs offering only kW-class or sub-MW single units (Horizon, Hyundai's 100 kW unit, Toshiba's base 100 kW module) would need many parallel units to reach data-center scale; only Plug Power's GenSure HP and Ballard's FCgen-XD/Cummins-Accelera's 1 MW unit are explicitly marketed at MW scale today.

### Gaps
- Siemens Energy: no public model/product name, kW rating, efficiency, footprint, or reference installation found for its PEMFC stationary offering — likely because it is offered as an integrated/engineered solution rather than a catalog product, or because Siemens Energy's stationary fuel cell activity is early-stage/limited compared to its gas turbine and electrolyzer businesses. Needs direct vendor engagement or datasheet request.
- Exact vendor/technology confirmation for the Microsoft 3 MW hydrogen fuel cell backup power plant was not obtained (article fetch blocked); needs a follow-up read of the full Data Center Dynamics article or Microsoft's own announcement.
- Precise location, commissioning date, and current operating status of the Cummins/Hydrogenics 1 MW South Korea refinery PEM plant were not confirmed beyond secondary summaries.
- No data was found on additional Chinese PEMFC stationary-power OEMs (e.g., SinoHytec, Refire, Sunrise Power) despite the market data indicating China as a leading APAC PEMFC-manufacturing base for stationary applications — out of scope of the explicitly-named vendors in the assignment, but worth flagging as an adjacent research gap for APAC coverage.

## For each OEM, what are the technical specs — power range, module/stack configuration, operating temperature, and H₂ purity/fuel spec?

### Takeaway
Confirmed hard numbers exist for Hyundai HTWO (100 kW/70 kW, ISO 14687-2/SAE J2719, 7–9 bar supply) and Ballard's FCgen-HPS stack (140 kW, 52% LHV efficiency); most other OEMs' stationary units are described in kW/MW ranges and modular architecture, but explicit operating-temperature figures per model were largely not published; the generic PEMFC operating temperature range (60–80 °C) and the >99.97% H₂ purity requirement are well-supported by industry/standards sources rather than every individual OEM datasheet.

### Cited Findings
- Generic PEMFC hydrogen fuel purity requirement: **>99.97% mole fraction**, per **ISO 14687** — "ISO 14687:2025, the international specification for hydrogen fuel quality, sets a minimum purity of 99.97% by mole fraction," with contaminant caps of CO ≤0.2 ppm, CO₂ ≤2 ppm, total sulfur ≤0.004 ppm, ammonia ≤0.1 ppm, total hydrocarbons ≤2 ppm — [ASTG — Hydrogen Fuel Purity Analysis to ISO 14687](https://astg.com/pages/hydrogen-fuel-purity-analysis-iso-14687); confirmed generically by [Stargate Hydrogen — Hydrogen Purity: How Pure Is Pure Enough?](https://stargatehydrogen.com/blog/hydrogen-purity/); DOE's own PEM fuel quality reference (fetch blocked, but cited by search index): [DOE FCTO — Hydrogen Fuel Quality Specifications for PEM Fuel Cells in Road Vehicles](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2016/11/f34/fcto_h2_fuel_quality_specs_pem_fc_road_vehicles.pdf) (note: this DOE document is scoped to road vehicles, not stationary power specifically — stationary PEMFC purity requirements are generally understood in industry literature to track the same ISO 14687 Type I Grade D spec, but a stationary-specific DOE target document was not independently retrieved in this session — **flagged as an assumption carried from the vehicle spec, not a confirmed stationary-specific citation**).
- Hyundai HTWO's own stationary generator datasheet specifies fuel per **ISO 14687-2 / SAE J2719** at **7–9 bar supply pressure** — [HTWO Fuel Cell Solution PDF](https://ecv.hyundai.com/resources/assets/download/HTWO_Fuel_Cell_Solution_ENG.pdf) — this is the one OEM-specific, sourced fuel-spec figure found for a stationary unit in this research pass.
- Ballard's **FCgen-HPS** stack: 140 kW output, 4.3 kW/L power density, **52% electrical efficiency at beginning of life (LHV)** — [Ballard FCgen-HPS specs PDF, Aug 2024](https://www.ballard.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Ballard_FCgen_HPS_specs.pdf). Note: this stack is used across multiple Ballard applications (the source did not confirm it is stationary-exclusive) — flagged, since the search summary described it under "various applications including stationary power," not as stationary-only.
- Ballard's dedicated stationary product family (FCgen-XD) spans **200 kW to MW-scale**; separately, Ballard's heavy-duty modules for stationary use are described as **70 kW–405 kW net power** — [Ballard Stationary Power Generation page](https://www.ballard.com/stationary-power-generation/); [FCgen-XD datasheet](https://info.ballard.com/hubfs/PDFs/FCgen-%20XD.pdf?hsLang=en-gb) — exact electrical efficiency figure for FCgen-XD specifically (as opposed to FCgen-HPS) was not found — **gap**.
- General PEMFC operating temperature is understood industry-wide as **~60–80 °C** (per project brief baseline, consistent with standard PEMFC electrochemistry); no OEM-specific operating-temperature figure (e.g., for GenSure, FCgen-XD, or H2Rex) was found published in the sources retrieved this session — **gap** (this is a category-level fact, not sourced per-OEM in this pass).
- Toshiba H2Rex module architecture: built as an array of identical **100 kW** modules (e.g., 5× 100 kW = 500 kW); base module also offered at **3.5 kW** for micro applications — [Toshiba news releases, 2020–2024, cited above].
- Plug Power GenSure HP module architecture: array of **125 kW ProGen** engines; product suite explicitly "designed for scalability... parallel operations providing increased power output... internal redundancy" — [Plug Power GenSure resource](https://resources.plugpower.com/gensure-stationary-power-fuel-cell).
- Cummins/Accelera Hawaii port unit: **100 kW system built from four 30 kW fuel cells** plus power-conversion equipment and 75 kg onboard H₂ storage — [Accelera stationary power page, per search summary](https://www.accelerazero.com/applications/stationary-power).

### Inferences
- The modular "many identical mid-size (30–140 kW) fuel cell units in parallel" architecture is essentially universal across Plug Power, Toshiba, and Cummins/Accelera's larger systems — this is a structural pattern relevant to MYSGH2 project sizing logic (a MW-scale stationary PEMFC plant is realistically built as N × ~100–140 kW parallel modules with built-in redundancy, not a single large stack).
- Because ISO 14687 purity requirements are set at the standard/fuel-quality level rather than per-OEM, any hydrogen delivered to a PEMFC stationary plant (including one fed by an ammonia cracker's product H₂ stream, as in the MYSGH2 project) would need to be purified/polished to ≥99.97% mol H₂ with tight CO/S/NH₃ ppm caps — this has direct relevance to the ammonia-cracker-to-PEMFC pathway, since cracker product gas (H₂/N₂ mixture, potential residual NH₃ slip) would require a purification step (e.g., PSA) before PEMFC use; note ISO 14687 caps ammonia at just **0.1 ppm**, meaning any NH₃ slip from imperfect ammonia cracking is a first-order PEMFC-fuel-quality risk for this project.

### Gaps
- Per-OEM operating temperature figures (as opposed to the generic PEMFC 60–80 °C range) were not found published for GenSure, FCgen-XD, H2Rex, or the Accelera stationary units in this research pass.
- A stationary-power-specific (as opposed to road-vehicle-specific) DOE or ISO fuel-quality document was not independently retrieved; the 99.97%/ISO 14687 figures used above are drawn from vehicle-application and general hydrogen-purity sources, on the reasonable but unconfirmed assumption that stationary PEMFC fuel-quality requirements track the same ISO 14687 baseline.
- Electrical efficiency figures (LHV or HHV, system-level rather than stack-level) were not found for Plug Power GenSure, Toshiba H2Rex, or Cummins/Accelera's MW-scale units — only Ballard's FCgen-HPS (52% LHV, stack BOL) and Hyundai HTWO (≥50% LHV, system-level) were sourced.

## What footprint, CAPEX $/kW, and stack lifetime data are publicly available?

### Takeaway
OEM-specific footprint (m²/MW) and $/kW installed-cost figures were not found in public sources during this research pass; only Hyundai HTWO's physical unit dimensions and general industry-level stack-lifetime and CAPEX ranges (not tied to a specific named OEM/product) were sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Hyundai HTWO 100 kW generator: fuel cell unit **1,300 × 1,500 × 2,300 mm**, PCS unit **1,000 × 1,500 × 2,340 mm**, each weighing **1,400 kg** — [HTWO Fuel Cell Solution PDF](https://ecv.hyundai.com/resources/assets/download/HTWO_Fuel_Cell_Solution_ENG.pdf). From these dimensions, the fuel cell unit alone occupies roughly 1.3 m × 1.5 m ≈ 1.95 m² of floor footprint for 100 kW (peak) — i.e., roughly **19.5 m² per MW** for the fuel cell module alone, **not counting the PCS unit, hydrogen storage/supply, balance-of-plant, or clearances** (this per-MW figure is a calculation derived from the sourced dimensions per Rule 7(3), not an OEM-published footprint metric — flag as a derived figure, not a directly cited one).
- Industry-wide (not OEM-specific) stationary PEMFC stack lifetime: reported range **60,000–90,000 hours**; a related figure states stack durability is expected to roughly double, "from 30,000 hours to 80,000 hours," alongside overall system life extending from a minimum of 6 years toward 20 years; a minimum commercially-viable stack lifetime for stationary PEM applications is cited as **≥40,000 hours (~5 years continuous operation)** — [IEA Fuel Cell — Stationary Fuel Cell Applications 2021: Current and Future Technologies](https://ieafuelcell.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2021-stationary-application-performance.pdf) (per search-index summary; direct PDF fetch not performed in this session — recommend a follow-up direct read for full context/exact wording).
- Industry-wide CAPEX for stationary fuel cell systems: **€2,000–€10,000/kW** across current technology types (not PEMFC-specific in this figure); a European projection targets **€1,500–€4,000/kW by 2030** for stationary systems; a separate micro-CHP program target cites **<€10,000/kWe CAPEX with 40,000–80,000-hour stack durability** — [Clean Hydrogen Partnership / EU stationary fuel cell presentation, "5.Stationary-Antonio Aguilo"](https://www.clean-hydrogen.europa.eu/system/files/2021-12/5.Stationary-Antonio%20Aguilo.pdf) (per search-index summary — figures are Euro-denominated, general "stationary fuel cell" (not exclusively PEMFC), and not tied to a single named OEM; direct PDF read not performed in this session — flag for follow-up verification of exact figures/years against the source PDF).
- A DOE manufacturing-cost analysis exists specifically for **5–10 kW PEM backup power systems** (a different, smaller product class than MW-scale gensets) — [DOE — Manufacturing Cost Analysis of PEM Fuel Cell Systems for 5-10kW Backup Power](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2016/12/f34/fcto_cost_analysis_pem_fc_5-10kw_backup_power_0.pdf) (title/existence found via search; content not fetched in this session due to egress restriction on energy.gov — **gap**, follow-up recommended).

### Inferences
- No OEM (Plug Power, Ballard, Cummins/Accelera, Hyundai, Toshiba) publishes a footprint-per-MW or $/kW-installed figure in open marketing/spec-sheet material found in this pass; these numbers are typically commercial-in-confidence and released only in RFP/vendor-quote processes. For MYSGH2 project sizing, footprint and CAPEX should be treated as **assumption/placeholder ranges pending vendor RFI**, bounded by the general stationary-fuel-cell industry ranges above (€2,000–10,000/kW today, trending to €1,500–4,000/kW by 2030), not as a PEMFC- or vendor-specific number.

### Gaps
- No OEM-specific, MW-scale footprint (m²/MW) figure was found for Plug Power GenSure HP, Ballard FCgen-XD, Cummins/Accelera 1 MW unit, or Toshiba H2Rex 500 kW array (beyond the single derived Hyundai calculation above, which itself excludes BOP/storage/PCS floor area).
- No OEM-specific $/kW installed cost was found for any of the six named companies; all cost data found is generic industry/DOE/EU program-level, not company- or product-specific.
- Full text of the IEA Fuel Cell 2021 stationary applications report and the EU Clean Hydrogen Partnership stationary presentation were not directly read (only search-engine summaries) — recommend direct fetch/read as a follow-up, since network egress blocked several primary-source domains (energy.gov, plugpower.com resource subdomain, datacenterdynamics.com, enkiai.com, accelerazero.com) during this research session.

## What is the commercial deployment track record, especially multi-MW backup/grid-support and APAC/Singapore/Malaysia installations?

### Takeaway
Confirmed commercial/reference deployments include Toshiba's H2Rex installs in Japan (Toyota Honsha Plant 100 kW, 2021; Tanaka Kikinzoku Shonan Plant 500 kW, 2024), a Cummins/Hydrogenics 1 MW PEM plant in South Korea, and Plug Power's pilot GenSure HP deployment at a large (unnamed) US data center; explicit Singapore/Malaysia PEMFC stationary-power deployments are sparse — Malaysia's documented case is a small legacy 3 kW telecom UPS pilot (Celcom, 2006), and Singapore's most concrete tie is Horizon Fuel Cell Technologies being headquartered there (manufacturer, not necessarily deploying in-country) plus a Horizon PEM system sold to South Korea, not Singapore itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Japan (Toshiba H2Rex)**: 100 kW unit operating at Toyota Motor Corporation's Honsha Plant since commissioning announced **November 2021** — [Toshiba news release, Nov 2021](https://www.global.toshiba/ww/news/energy/2021/11/news-20211130-01.html); 500 kW system (5× 100 kW) ordered by Tanaka Kikinzoku Kogyo for its Shonan Plant, announced **March 2024** — [Toshiba news release, Mar 2024](https://www.global.toshiba/ww/news/energy/2024/03/news-20240313-01.html); a 3.5 kW unit installed at the Michinoeki-Namie roadside station in Fukushima, delivered **August 2020** — [Toshiba news release, Aug 2020](https://www.global.toshiba/ww/news/energy/2020/08/news-20200803-01.html); an additional install for Shimizu Corporation's hydrogen energy system, announced **May 2021** — [Toshiba news release, May 2021](https://www.global.toshiba/ww/news/energy/2021/05/news-20210514-01.html).
